Swim splits and the split times table

A split is the clock reading as you pass a point in the race. Swimmers use them two ways: the cumulative time, which is what the scoreboard shows, and the individual split, which is what that particular 50 actually took. The calculator gives you both, because coaches ask for both.

This swimming split calculator turns one target time into split times for every wall you choose.

The full table appears below the calculator once you enter a time. Read down the cumulative column to know what the clock should say as you push off each wall, and down the split column to know how fast each length has to be.

The three pacing plans

Same target time, three different ways to arrive at it. None is universally right — the event decides.

Even

Every split identical. It is the most efficient way to cover a distance, it is the easiest to rehearse in training, and it is what most distance swimmers should aim for. It is also the baseline the other two are measured against.

In practice nobody swims perfectly even, because the first split includes a dive and the last includes a finish. Treat it as the shape, not the instruction.

Negative split

Second half faster than the first. This plan holds back about one per cent in the first half and spends it in the second. It is the classic way to swim a 400 or further, and it is how most distance world records are set.

The reason it works is that going out too fast costs you more than it gains. Lactate accumulates faster than you can clear it, and the back half falls apart. Starting a fraction slow keeps that from happening.

Race profile

This is the shape an actual race takes: a quick first split from the dive, a settle, a steady middle, and a lift on the last one. It is not a strategy so much as a description — it is what good swimmers do without thinking about it.

Use it to check whether a target is realistic. If the first split it asks for is faster than you have ever gone off a dive, the target is too ambitious.

Rehearse the splits, do not just read them. A target split only helps if you can feel it. Swim the set at that pace with the clock in front of you until the number and the feeling match — that is what makes it repeatable on race day.

How to calculate swim split times

Start with even pacing: count the splits (race distance ÷ split length), then divide the target time by that number. A 4:00 400 split every 50 is 240 seconds ÷ 8 = 30.00 a split. Negative split and race profile plans move time between the splits while the total stays the same.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate swim splits?

For even pacing, divide your target time by the number of splits. A 2:30 200 split every 50 is 150 seconds over four, which is 37.50 each. Cumulative splits are the running total: 37.50, 1:15.00, 1:52.50, 2:30.00.

What is a negative split in swimming?

Swimming the second half of a race faster than the first. It usually means holding back about one per cent early so you have something left to spend late. Most distance records are swum this way.

Should my first 50 be the fastest?

In anything up to a 200, yes — the dive makes it faster whether you plan it or not. From the 400 up, a first 50 that is much quicker than the rest is usually a sign you went out too hard.

What is the difference between a split and a cumulative time?

The cumulative time is the clock reading at that point in the race. The split is how long that section took on its own. A 1:15.00 cumulative at the 100 of a 200 with a 37.50 first 50 means the second 50 split was 37.50.

How much slower should the first half be for a negative split?

About one per cent either side of even is a sensible target, which is what this calculator uses. Much more than two per cent and you are not negative splitting, you are starting too slowly and leaving time in the pool.

Can I use these splits in training?

Yes, and that is the best use for them. Take the target split, swim repeats at exactly that number with the clock in front of you, and keep going until it feels automatic. A split you can hit on demand in training is one you can hit in a race.

Does the swim split calculator work for yards?

Yes. Splits depend on the distance and the target time, so choose 200, 500, 1000 or 1650 and treat the results as yard splits. The 100, 200 and 400 work in either unit.

Can I predict a 50 time from a 25 split?

Not by simply doubling it. The first 25 includes the start dive and is faster than the second, so doubling it gives a quicker 50 than you are likely to swim. The calculator starts at 100.
