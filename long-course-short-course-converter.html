How to convert long course to short course

A long course time is swum in a 50-metre pool. A short course time is swum in a 25-metre or 25-yard pool, where you get more walls — and a wall is faster than swimming. Converting means estimating what that same swim would have been worth in the shorter pool.

The method here is the USA Swimming turn-value set built into Hy-Tek Team Manager, which is what most meets seed from. It takes the seconds off for the walls you gain, and where the distance also changes it divides by a course factor.

There are two short courses

This is the step most people miss. "Short course" means two different things depending on where you swim, and the conversion is not the same for both.

  • SCM — short course metres, a 25-metre pool. The distance is identical to long course, so the conversion is pure turn value. Nothing is multiplied.
  • SCY — short course yards, a 25-yard pool. The lane is 22.86 m, so the race is shorter as well. You take the walls off and then divide by the course factor.

What changes between 50 m and 25 m

Only the walls. A 100 long course has one turn; the same 100 short course has three. A 200 goes from three turns to seven. Every extra wall is a push-off and an underwater, both quicker than swimming on the surface, so the short course time comes out faster.

Long course to short course conversion factors

One number per stroke, applied once for every wall you gain. Backstroke gains least, because backstroke swimming speed is already close to underwater speed. Breaststroke gains most, because the pull-out is far quicker than the stroke.

LCM to SCM — walls only

A 50-metre pool and a 25-metre pool race the same distance, so there is no factor at all. Subtract the turn value and you are done.

SCM = LCM − turn value

200 breaststroke, 2:32.40 long course. 152.40 s − (4 walls × 1.0 s) = 148.40 s = 2:28.40 SCM.

LCM to SCY — walls, then divide

A 25-yard pool is shorter than 25 metres, so after the walls you divide by the course factor — 1.11 for the 50, 100, 200 and 400 IM.

SCY = (LCM − turn value) ÷ 1.11

100 backstroke, 1:08.00 long course. 68.00 − 1.2 = 66.80. 66.80 ÷ 1.11 = 60.18 SCY.

Distance events are the exception. A 400 long course maps to a 500 yards, not a 400 yards, so it uses 0.8925 and no turn value. The 1500 maps to the 1650 and uses 1.02. The converter handles this for you.

Short course to long course

Going the other way, you are losing walls, so the time gets slower. Reverse each operation and the order: multiply first, then add the seconds.

LCM = SCM + turn value  ·  LCM = (SCY × 1.11) + turn value

100 freestyle, 52.30 SCY. 52.30 × 1.11 = 58.05, which is your short course metres time. Add 1.6 for the two walls lost: 59.65 long course.

That single example contains both conversions — yards to metres, then short course to long course. It is why the 25-yard time and the 50-metre time look so far apart even though the swimmer never changed.

Why a converted short course time can flatter you

The turn value is one number for everyone. In reality a wall is worth whatever your underwater is worth, and that varies enormously. A swimmer who breaks out at twelve metres gains far more from two extra walls than one who surfaces at five.

So if your underwaters are a strength, a long-course-to-short-course conversion will usually under-sell you, and you will swim faster than the number. If they are a weakness, the converted time is optimistic. Expect the estimate to land within about two to three per cent either way.

Converted times also are not records, and championship meets normally want a time actually swum in the course they are run in. For seeding at a club or masters meet, a conversion is usually accepted — their software produces it from this same table.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert long course to short course?

Subtract the turn value for every wall you gain. Going from a 50-metre pool to a 25-metre pool, a 100 freestyle gains two walls at 0.8 seconds each, so you take off 1.6 seconds. For a 25-yard pool, take the seconds off first and then divide by 1.11.

Is short course always faster than long course?

For the same distance, yes. A 100 in a 25-metre pool gives you two more walls than a 100 long course, and a push-off is faster than swimming, so the short course time is lower. How much lower depends on the stroke — 1.2 seconds for backstroke, 2.0 for breaststroke.

What is the difference between SCM and SCY?

SCM is a 25-metre pool and SCY is a 25-yard pool, which is 22.86 metres. Both are short course, but only SCM races the same distance as long course. That is why converting long course to SCM needs no factor, while converting to SCY needs the 1.11 divide as well.

How do I convert LCM to SCY?

Take off the turn value for the walls you gain, then divide by 1.11. A 1:08.00 long course 100 backstroke becomes 68.00 − 1.2 = 66.80, divided by 1.11, which is 60.18 short course yards. The distance events use 0.8925 or 1.02 instead and carry no turn value.

How many seconds do you take off for a 200?

A 200 gains four walls between long course and short course. That is 2.4 seconds for backstroke, 2.8 for butterfly, 3.2 for freestyle and medley, and 4.0 for breaststroke.

Will a meet accept a converted long course time?

For seeding at most club and masters meets, yes — their entry software converts from this same table. Championship qualifying times and official standards usually require a time swum in that course, and a converted time never counts as a record. Check the meet information sheet.
