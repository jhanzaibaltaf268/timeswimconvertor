The British method is not the American one

Most swim time converters online use the USA Swimming method: multiply by a factor, then add a flat number of seconds for each wall. British Swimming uses something different, and it gives different answers.

Use this page as an SC to LC swimming converter, or switch the direction to go from long course to short course.

The British approach treats the turn advantage as something that shrinks as you get slower. Two swimmers in the same 100 freestyle do not gain the same amount from the two extra walls in a 25-metre pool — the faster one gains more. The American method hands both of them the same 1.6 seconds.

Which should a UK swimmer use? For a Swim England county, regional or national entry, the British algorithm is the relevant one, because that is what the official converters are built on. The American method is fine for a rough comparison and for anything involving a yards pool, which British Swimming does not cover at all.

The equivalent time formula

Each event has a turn factor, TF. Distance d is in metres and times are in decimal seconds.

LCM = [ TSC + √( TSC² + 8 × TF × (d/100)² ) ] ÷ 2

SCM = TLC − (TF ÷ TLC) × (d/100)² × 2

Both are rounded to a tenth of a second. They are exact inverses of each other, so converting there and back returns your original time.

Why your speed changes the gain

Look at the TF ÷ T term. The slower your time, the smaller that fraction, and the less the turns are worth. That is the whole difference between the two systems, and over a 100 freestyle it looks like this:

Worked example: convert short course to long course

A 100 freestyle in 1:00.0 in a 25-metre pool. The turn factor is 42.245 and (d/100)² is 1. So LCM = [60 + √(60² + 8 × 42.245)] ÷ 2 = 61.4 seconds, which is 1:01.40 long course.

Using a converted time for entries

Meet organisers set their own conversion code, so the rule varies. The usual pattern is that you compare your best time in the course being swum against your converted time from the other course, and enter whichever is faster.

A converted time gets you seeded. It does not become a personal best, it does not appear as a result on the Swim England rankings, and it cannot be used where a meet explicitly asks for a time achieved in that course.

Before entering anything that matters, check the meet conditions and run the time through the official converter on the Swim England rankings site. This page implements the published algorithm and matches those converters, but the official source is the one that counts.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert short course to long course in the UK?

UK clubs use the British Swimming equivalent time algorithm, which takes your short course time, adds eight times the event's turn factor multiplied by the squared distance in hundreds, takes the square root and halves the result. The calculator above does it for you — enter the time and pick the event.

Is the ASA conversion the same as the Swim England one?

Yes. The ASA became Swim England in 2017, and the conversion method carried over unchanged. Older club documents still call it the ASA equivalent performance conversion, and the tables and the algorithm behind it are the same thing.

Why does my club's converter give a different number to an American one?

They use different models. USA Swimming adds a flat number of seconds per wall regardless of how fast you are. British Swimming scales the turn advantage with your speed, so slower swimmers get less of it. On a 100 freestyle the two can differ by half a second or more.

Can I convert a yards time with this?

No. The British algorithm covers 25-metre and 50-metre pools only, which is almost every pool in the UK. For a 25-yard time, use the main swim time converter, which handles all three courses with the American method.

Does a converted time count as a personal best?

No. It is an estimate used for entry and seeding where the meet conditions allow it. It does not go on the rankings database and it is not a result. Only a time actually swum in that course is a personal best.

Which direction loses time?

Going from short course to long course always makes the time slower, because you lose walls. A 25-metre pool gives twice as many turns as a 50-metre pool over the same distance, and a push-off is faster than swimming.

How do I convert long course to short course with the UK method?

Choose LCM as the starting course and SCM as the target. The algorithm subtracts twice the turn factor divided by your long course time, multiplied by the squared distance in hundreds.

Is this a Swim England swim time converter?

It is an independent swim time conversion calculator that applies the British Swimming equivalent time algorithm. It is not run by or endorsed by Swim England, so check official entries against the source named in your meet conditions.
