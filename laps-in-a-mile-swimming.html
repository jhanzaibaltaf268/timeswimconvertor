How Many Laps Is a Mile Swimming?

Pick your pool and your distance. The answer depends on both — and on what you mean by a lap.

A length is one end of the pool to the other. Most swimmers say "lap" to mean the same thing — see why that matters.

The short answer

A mile is 1609.34 metres, or 1760 yards. How many lengths that takes depends entirely on the pool you are standing in — and in most pools it does not come out as a whole number.

  • 25-yard pool: 70.4 lengths. Most swimmers round to 70 or swim 72 for a round 18 hundreds.
  • 25-metre pool: 64.4 lengths, usually rounded to 64.
  • 50-metre pool: 32.2 lengths, usually rounded to 32.

If someone at your pool says "the mile", though, they almost certainly do not mean 1609 metres. They mean the race called the mile, which is a different distance again — covered below.

Lap or length?

This is the reason the same question gets two different answers online.

  • A length is unambiguous: one end of the pool to the other, once.
  • A lap means one length to most swimmers and most coaches, which is how the word is used in practice and in this calculator.
  • But a lap in running means a full circuit back to where you started, and some pools, apps and lifeguards keep that meaning — there and back, so two lengths.

If a number looks twice what you expected, that is why. The calculator shows both so you can take whichever your pool means.

Only the 20-yard pool divides evenly, because 1760 yards is exactly 88 twenties. Everything else leaves you part way down the pool, which is precisely why racing settled on distances that do divide.

Why the swim mile is not a mile

Competitive swimming has two events people call the mile, and neither is one.

The 1650 in yards

In a 25-yard pool the distance event is the 1650 freestyle — 66 lengths, a round 16½ hundreds. That is 1650 yards, which is 1508.76 metres, around 100 metres short of a true mile. It got the nickname because it is the closest standard race, not because it measures one.

The 1500 in metres

In metre pools the distance event is the 1500 freestyle, sometimes called the metric mile. It is 60 lengths of a 25-metre pool or 30 of a 50, and at 1500 metres it is about 109 metres short of a mile.

So three different things get called the mile: the true mile at 1609.34 m, the 1650-yard race at 1508.76 m, and the 1500-metre race. If a training plan or a swim event says "mile", check which one it means before you set off.

How to count without losing track

Seventy lengths is a lot to hold in your head, and most people lose count somewhere in the forties. A few things that work:

  • Count in hundreds, not lengths. In a 25 pool, four lengths is one hundred. Counting to 18 is far easier than counting to 72.
  • Break it into sets. Six 300s with a few seconds rest is the same distance as a straight 1800 and much easier to track.
  • Use the pace clock. If you know your pace per 100, the clock tells you roughly where you are even when the count has gone.
  • Lane counters. Every serious distance set in a meet uses one for a reason.

Frequently asked questions

How many laps is a mile in a 25-yard pool?

70.4 lengths, since a mile is 1760 yards. Most swimmers round to 70, or swim 72 for a tidy 1800 yards. If your pool counts a lap as there and back, that is 35.2 laps.

How many lengths is a mile in a 25-metre pool?

64.4 lengths, because a mile is 1609.34 metres. Most people swim 64 and call it a mile, which is 1600 metres — nine metres short.

How many lengths is a mile in a 50-metre pool?

32.2 lengths, normally rounded to 32, which is 1600 metres. In a 50-metre pool a length and a there-and-back lap are easy to confuse, so 16 laps also means a mile if your pool counts both directions as one.

Is the 1650 a mile?

No. The 1650-yard freestyle is 66 lengths of a 25-yard pool and measures 1508.76 metres, about 100 metres short of a true mile. It is nicknamed the mile because it is the closest standard race in a yards pool.

Is a lap one length or two?

In swimming, a lap almost always means one length — down the pool once. The there-and-back meaning comes from running and athletics, and some pools and fitness apps still use it. When a number looks doubled, that is usually the reason.

How long does it take to swim a mile?

At a 2:00 per 100 m pace a mile takes about 32 minutes. At 1:40 it is about 27 minutes, at 1:30 around 24, and a competitive 1500 is under 17. Multiply your pace per 100 by 16.1 for a 1609-metre mile.
