What FINA points are

FINA points put every swim on one scale. A 50 breaststroke and a 1500 freestyle, a man and a woman, a 50-metre pool and a 25-metre pool — all of them come out as a single number you can compare directly. A thousand points is a world-record swim. Everything else sits below it.

That is why meets use them to rank finals across events, why clubs use them to pick relay squads, and why swimmers use them to tell whether their 200 fly is really better than their 100 free.

FINA points or World Aquatics points?

The same thing. FINA renamed itself World Aquatics in 2022, so the official name is now World Aquatics Point Scoring. Most swimmers, meet programmes and software still say FINA points, and both terms refer to the identical table.

The formula

One line, and it has not changed in years.

P = 1000 × (B ÷ T)³

P is your points, B is the official base time for that event in seconds, and T is your time in seconds. The result is truncated down to a whole number.

The cube is the part that surprises people. Points do not fall away in a straight line as your time gets slower — they fall away faster and faster. Swim ten per cent slower than the base time and you do not score 900, you score about 751.

Where the base time comes from

The base time for each event is the world record approved by World Aquatics at the start of the scoring period. Match it exactly and you score 1000.

The two courses are set on different dates, which is why the tables have different validity windows:

  • Long course base times are fixed at the end of the calendar year and run for the next calendar year.
  • Short course base times are fixed at the end of August and run from 1 September for twelve months.

Because records keep falling, the same swim scores slightly fewer points each time the table is refreshed. A score is therefore always tied to the year it was calculated in.

There is no yards table. World Aquatics points exist for 50-metre and 25-metre pools only. If your time is from a 25-yard pool, convert it to metres first and score the converted time — and remember the conversion is itself an estimate.

What your score means

There is no official grading, but the scale is used consistently enough that these bands are a fair guide for an individual swim.

How FINA point scores are generally read.
PointsRoughly
900+World class — finals at a World Championships or Olympics
800–900International level, national champion territory
700–800Strong national level, national qualifying times
600–700Regional standard, competitive club swimmer
500–600Solid club level
400–500Club swimmer, early competitive
Below 400Developing or recreational

Points say nothing about age. A fourteen-year-old and a thirty-year-old with the same time score the same, so the number compares performances, not potential. For age-group context you want time standards, not points.

A worked example

A man swims 53.50 in the 100 freestyle, long course. The 2026 base time for that event is 46.40 seconds.

P = 1000 × (46.40 ÷ 53.50)³

46.40 ÷ 53.50 = 0.86729. Cubed, that is 0.65237. Multiplied by 1000 and truncated: 652 points.

To go the other way — the time you need for a target score — rearrange it:

T = B ÷ (P ÷ 1000)^⅓

For 700 points in the same event: 46.40 ÷ 0.8879 = 52.26.

The calculator shows that ladder automatically, so you can see what 500, 600, 700 and 800 points look like in your own event.

Frequently asked questions

What are FINA points?

A single scale for comparing swims across different events, distances, strokes, genders and pool sizes. Each event has an official base time worth 1000 points, and your swim scores 1000 × (base ÷ your time)³. They are now officially called World Aquatics points.

How are FINA points calculated?

Divide the base time by your time, cube the result, multiply by 1000 and round down. For a 53.50 in the men's long course 100 freestyle against a 46.40 base, that is 1000 × (46.40 ÷ 53.50)³ = 652 points.

What is a good FINA points score?

Over 900 is world class. 700 to 800 is strong national level. 500 to 600 is a solid club swimmer, and 400 to 500 is early competitive. There is no official grading, but these bands are used consistently across the sport.

How often do the base times change?

Once a year for each course. Long course base times are set at the end of December and run for the calendar year. Short course base times are set at the end of August and run from 1 September. Because records fall, the same swim scores slightly fewer points under each new table.

Can I get FINA points for a yards time?

Not directly. World Aquatics publishes base times for 50-metre and 25-metre pools only. Convert your short course yards time to short course metres first, then score the converted time — but treat the result as an estimate, because the conversion is one too.

Can you score more than 1000 points?

Yes. The base time is the world record at the start of the scoring period, so any swim faster than it scores above 1000. Published points tables run past 1100 for exactly that reason.
