How the swim time converter works

A 100 free in a 25-yard pool and a 100 free in a 50-metre pool are two different races. The distance is different, and so is the number of walls you get to push off. Converting a swim time means estimating what the same effort would have produced in the other pool.

This tool applies the turn-value method published by USA Swimming and built into Hy-Tek Team Manager, the software that seeds most meets in the United States. It multiplies your time by a course factor to handle the change in distance, then adds or subtracts a fixed number of seconds for every wall you gain or lose.

SCY, SCM and LCM explained

Competitive swimming runs in three pool sizes. Drawn to the same scale, the difference is obvious — a 50-metre pool is more than twice the length of a 25-yard one.

Because a yard is 0.9144 metres, 100 yards is only 91.44 metres. That alone makes a metre race roughly nine per cent longer, which is where the familiar 1.11 factor comes from.

Why pool length changes your time

Distance is only half the story. The other half is walls. A 100 in a short course pool has three turns; the same 100 long course has one. A turn is a push-off and an underwater, and both are faster than swimming on the surface — so every wall you lose costs you time.

That is why a long course time is slower than the short course equivalent even after you adjust for distance, and why the penalty is not the same for every stroke. Breaststroke turns are worth the most, because the pull-out is far quicker than the stroke itself. Backstroke turns are worth the least.

How to convert a swim time by hand

Work in total seconds, apply the factor, then the walls, then convert back to minutes and seconds.

Yards to metres (SCY to LCM)

LCM = (SCY × factor) + turn value

100 freestyle, 52.30 SCY. 52.30 × 1.11 = 58.05. Two walls lost × 0.8 s = 1.6. Result: 59.65 LCM.

Short course to long course (SCM to LCM)

Here the distance is already identical, so there is no factor — only the walls.

LCM = SCM + turn value

200 breaststroke, 2:28.40 SCM. 148.40 s + (4 walls × 1.0 s) = 152.40 s = 2:32.40 LCM.

Long course to short course

Reverse the order: take the walls off first, then divide by the factor.

SCY = (LCM − turn value) ÷ factor

100 backstroke, 1:08.00 LCM. 68.00 − 1.2 = 66.80. 66.80 ÷ 1.11 = 60.18 SCY.

How accurate are converted swim times?

A converted time is an estimate, not a result. The turn-value method applies one number per stroke to every swimmer, but the value of a wall is personal: a swimmer with a 12-metre underwater gains far more from short course than one who surfaces at five metres.

Expect a converted time to land within roughly two to three per cent of what you would actually swim. For context, the same swimmer in the same pool typically varies by around two per cent between meets a few weeks apart, so no conversion method can be meaningfully tighter than that.

Where conversions drift most: strong underwater swimmers converting short course to long course, breaststrokers at any distance, and the 1650 to 1500 freestyle, where the two races are a different length as well as a different course.

When meets accept a converted time

Entry rules belong to the meet, not to the converter. Most US club and masters meets accept converted times for seeding, because Hy-Tek produces them from this same table. Championship meets and time standards are usually stricter and want a time swum in the course they are run in.

Converted times are also not records. A converted swim can seed you into a heat; it cannot set a mark. If you are entering a qualifying meet, check the meet information sheet before relying on a conversion.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert swim times from yards to meters?

Multiply your short course yards time by 1.11 to get short course metres, then add the turn value for your stroke and distance to reach long course metres. For a 100 freestyle that is your time × 1.11 + 1.6 seconds. The converter above does both steps at once.

Why is the swim conversion factor 1.11?

A yard is 0.9144 metres, so a metre race is about 9.4 per cent longer. The factor is slightly above that because a yards pool also gives you more walls per 100, and the extra push-offs make yards times faster than distance alone explains.

How much slower is long course than short course?

For a 100, roughly one to two seconds between 25-metre and 50-metre pools, depending on stroke: 1.2 seconds for backstroke, 1.6 for freestyle, 2.0 for breaststroke. Between 25 yards and 50 metres the gap is much larger, because the distance changes too.

Why is backstroke different from the other strokes?

Backstroke carries the smallest turn value, 0.6 seconds per wall, because backstroke swimming speed is closer to underwater speed than in the other strokes. Breaststroke carries the largest, 1.0 second, since the pull-out is far faster than the stroke.

Which conversion factors does this tool use?

The USA Swimming turn-value set used by Hy-Tek Team Manager: a 1.11 course factor for the 50, 100, 200 and 400 IM, 0.8925 for 500 yards to 400 metres and 1000 to 800, 1.02 for 1650 to 1500, and turn values of 0.8 s freestyle, 0.6 s backstroke, 1.0 s breaststroke, 0.7 s butterfly and 0.8 s IM.

Is the swim time converter free?

Yes. There is no sign-up and no limit on conversions. Everything runs in your browser, so your times are never sent anywhere.
